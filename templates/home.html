What the numbers mean
The score out of 10 is a percentile, not a probability. It is where the predicted affinity falls inside the range actually measured against this isoform in ChEMBL. 7.0 means stronger than 70% of its known inhibitors. 3.2 means stronger than only 32% of them, so weaker than most of them. It says nothing about safety, selectivity, or whether the molecule can be made.
0 weakest5 typical known inhibitor10 strongest
Potency is that same prediction as a concentration: nM is how much of the molecule you would need, so lower is better. pAff underneath is −log10 of it in molar, pooling IC50 and Ki the way ChEMBL does.
Lipinski’s Rule of 5 asks four questions about whether a molecule could work as a pill: molecular weight ≤ 500, LogP ≤ 5, hydrogen-bond donors ≤ 5, acceptors ≤ 10. Each chip shows the measured value and turns amber when it breaks its limit. Lipinski’s own formulation tolerates one violation, which is why a molecule failing a single rule still counts as drug-like. QED rolls the same idea into one 0–1 number, where 0.5 and above is typical of marketed oral drugs.

How the model and the docking work, and what they cannot tell you
Potency comes from a Random Forest and LSTM ensemble over the molecule’s fingerprint plus four descriptors of the target protein, which is why the same molecule scores differently against different isoforms. Trained on {{ '{:,}'.format(family_measurements) }} measurements from ChEMBL, split by molecule so that no molecule appears in both training and test.
An isoform is only scored if the model demonstrably predicts it — enough measured molecules and a usable R² on held-out molecules of that isoform. Where it does not, the structure can still be docked and its site inspected, and the number is withheld rather than guessed.

Docking uses AutoDock Vina with the AutoDock4Zn force field. The receptor is protonated and its zinc maps built for your structure on demand, so the first molecule takes about a minute. Read the zinc distance, not just the score: these inhibitors work by coordinating the catalytic zinc with a sulfonamide nitrogen at roughly 2 Å, so a pose that scores well but sits 5 Å away is probably not the real binding mode. The receptor is rigid and the solvent implicit, which makes the scores a ranking aid rather than binding free energies.
